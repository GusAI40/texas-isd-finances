Why this exists

Public schools are the biggest thing your Texas taxes pay for. You should be able to see inside.

For most Texas families, school property taxes are the single largest line on the tax bill — thousands of dollars a year. Yet what that money actually does, whether it's working, and how your district compares is buried in state spreadsheets almost nobody reads. This guide exists to change that.

Our mission: bring plain-English transparency to how every Texas school district raises, spends, and borrows public money — and what it buys for students — using the state's own official records, free, for anyone, with a source on every number.

The problem

The numbers that decide how your schools are funded — budgets, tax rates, recapture, debt, test results, teacher pay and turnover, bond elections — are all public. But "public" and "readable" are not the same thing. They live in dozens of separate state files, in the language of accountants and agencies. A parent choosing a neighborhood, a taxpayer wondering where the money went, or a reporter on deadline can't reasonably piece them together. So almost no one does.

What we did about it

We gathered 17 years of the Texas Education Agency's and Comptroller's own records — every district's audited finances, property values and tax bills, STAAR results, teacher data, and every school bond back to 1958 — 6.6 million data points in all. Then we translated them into plain English: what your district collects, where each dollar goes, what it borrowed and for what, and what the spending actually bought in results. Every figure links back to the state source it came from. Nothing is estimated, ranked, or spun.

Why you should care

  • If you're a parent — see what a district actually delivers for students before you choose a neighborhood, a home, or a school. Ratings mostly reflect how wealthy a community already is; this shows what the district itself does with what it has.
  • If you're a taxpayer or grandparent — see exactly where your school taxes go, how much leaves your district under recapture, and whether the spending is buying results.
  • If you're a teacher — see pay, class sizes, and turnover against genuinely comparable districts, not the whole state average.
  • If you're a reporter — a sourced, district-by-district starting point for any story, with the receipts already attached.
  • If you're a school board member or trustee — benchmark your district against its real structural peers and see what actually moves outcomes.

What we stand for

  • Independent. Not a TEA product, not affiliated with any district, and nothing to sell. The numbers are the state's; the plain-English framing is ours.
  • Sourced. Every figure traces back to an official record. If we can't source it, we don't show it.
  • Honest, even when it's inconvenient. We report what the data says — including that spending $2,000 more per student changed results by too little to tell apart from zero, and that some celebrated turnarounds left their most vulnerable students behind.
  • No accusations, no rankings. Anomaly flags are questions worth asking, not findings of wrongdoing. We never rank or endorse a district or community.
  • Privacy first. District-level aggregates only — no student, family, or personnel data is collected, stored, or shown. "Find my district" on the map runs entirely in your browser. We measure the site, never the visitor — here is exactly what that means.
  • Open. The whole project is open source (MIT), so anyone can check our work.

What we collect (and what we don't)

We want to know whether this site is useful and what people come here to find out. That does not require knowing who you are, so we don't collect it. There are no cookies on this site, no advertising, no third-party trackers, and nothing is ever sold or shared.

  • Page views, as daily totals. When a page loads we add 1 to a counter for that page, that day, whether the screen was a phone or a desktop, and which site you came from (the site's name only, like "google.com" — never the full link). That's the whole record. There is no row for your visit and no clock time, so two people who read the same page today are indistinguishable in our data.
  • Questions you type into "Ask anything." We keep the question itself, because what Texans want to know about their schools is what should shape this site. It is stored on its own — with no IP address, no device details, and no ID of any kind — so a question can never be traced back to you, or linked to another question you asked. Please don't type personal details into it; there's no reason to, and we'd rather not have them.
  • What we never collect: your IP address, cookies, a visitor or session ID, your precise location, or anything that could fingerprint your browser. We don't track you across other websites, because there is nothing here that could.
  • Your location stays on your device. "Find my district" uses your browser's location to work out which district you're standing in, entirely inside your browser. That location is never sent to us or to anyone else.
