# Tutorial — Using the Texas ISD Finance Dashboard

**Who this is for:** anyone. If you can use a search bar, you can use this.
The dashboard also has a built-in 60-second guided tour — click **🎓
Tutorial** in the top-right corner any time.

**The site:** https://txisd.dev

## 1. Pick your district (10 seconds)

Type a few letters of the district name — "alamo", "katy", "dallas" — and
click the right one from the list. The dashboard remembers your pick on
this device, so next visit it loads automatically. To look at a different
district (a neighbor, a rival, anyone), just search again.

## 2. Read your four headline tiles

| Tile | What it tells you |
|---|---|
| **Spending per student** | Total spending ÷ students, with the change vs last year and the statewide median right underneath for instant context |
| **Statewide rank** | "Spends more per student than 62% of Texas districts" — one sentence, no math needed |
| **Enrollment** | Fall student count and whether it's growing or shrinking |
| **Budget balance** | Did revenue cover spending this year? (One year alone is not a verdict — check the trend.) |

## 3. Trends — the gray dashed line is your friend

The chart shows 17 years of your district. The **gray dashed line is the
statewide median** — the "typical Texas district." If your line sits above
it, you spend more than typical; below, less. Use the tabs to switch
between spending per student, enrollment, and revenue vs. spending. **Hover
any dot** for exact numbers. The same numbers appear in the table below the
chart (that's also what the CSV button downloads).

**Finding patterns like a pro:** look for lines that *cross* (spending
crossing above revenue = deficits starting), sudden jumps (usually a bond
or one-time grant — check the money breakdown), and steady drift away from
the median (a structural difference worth understanding).

## 4. Compare with districts your size

Comparing a 400-student district to Houston ISD is meaningless. The peer
chart shows only the ~12 Texas districts **closest to your enrollment**.
Your bar is blue; the dashed line is the state median. The sentence under
the chart says your exact rank in plain English.

## 5. Where the money goes

Four bars: classrooms (instruction), debt payments, building projects, and
everything else (buses, food, utilities, administration). Statewide,
instruction is typically the biggest share — if debt or construction
dominates in some year, the trend chart usually shows the bond program that
explains it.

## 6. Anomaly check — flags are questions, not verdicts

If a number jumped in a way our automatic checks find unusual, a card
explains **what tripped the flag** and — importantly — the **innocent
explanations to rule out first** (expired grants, bond programs, boundary
changes). No flags = "numbers moved within normal ranges."

## 7. Take it to a board meeting

- **🖨 Print** (top right) — clean printout / save-as-PDF, charts included.
- **⬇ Download CSV** — the trend table, ready for Excel or Sheets.

## 8. Ask anything

The box at the bottom takes plain English: *"How did our debt service
change since 2020?"* An AI translates it into a database query against the
same read-only data. Double-check anything important against the charts or
[official TEA releases](https://tea.texas.gov/finance-and-grants/state-funding/state-funding-reports-and-data/peims-financial-data-downloads).

## Things to keep in mind

- Data is TEA's official summarized PEIMS release, fiscal 2009–2025;
  district-level totals only — no student or staff personal data.
- Very small districts show extreme per-student numbers (dividing by few
  students). That's arithmetic, not scandal.
- This is an independent transparency tool, not an official TEA product.
  Full disclaimer: **ⓘ Disclaimer** button on the dashboard.
