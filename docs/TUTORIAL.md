# Tutorial — Using the Texas ISD Finance Report

**Who this is for:** anyone. If you can use a search bar, you can use this.
The site also has a built-in guided tour — click **Tutorial** in the
top-right corner any time. The tour adapts to the district you are looking
at, so it never walks you to a section that district does not have.

**The site:** https://txisd.dev

**What it is made of:** 6.6 million data points from six official sources —
TEA's summarized PEIMS financial data (fiscal 2009–2025), TEA Snapshot,
district STAAR results, TEA/Comptroller certified property values and tax
rates, recapture payments back to 1994, bond election results back to 1958,
and Census district boundaries.

## Finding your way around

Two things sit above every page. The **thin bar across the very top** shows
how far through the page you are. The **row of chips** under it names each
section, highlights the one you are reading, and jumps to any of them.
**J** and **K** move section to section from the keyboard.

## 1. Pick your district (10 seconds)

Type a few letters — "alamo", "katy", "dallas" — and pick from the list.
The site remembers your choice on this device. To look at a different
district, just search again. You can also find yourself geographically on
the [map](https://txisd.dev/geomap).

## 2. The answer first

Spending per student against the state, your rank among districts your
size, enrollment direction, and whether revenue covered spending. Every
headline on the page states a finding, not a label.

## 3. Your dollar, as 100 pennies

Every dollar the district spends, split into real TEA function-code
categories. Hover or tap a colour for what it covers and how it compares
with structural peers.

## 4. What the money buys

Teachers, class sizes, attendance, graduation and test results — each
against districts with the same student need, and against what those
demographics predict.

**Read the bar carefully.** TEA publishes three: *Approaches*, *Meets* and
*Masters*. Statewide in 2024-25, **74.2%** of students reached Approaches
but only **46.5%** reached **Meets**. Approaches is not grade level; Meets
is. **This report uses Meets throughout**, and shows the other two as
context. Anyone quoting a number in the seventies is quoting the lowest bar.

## 5. What you pay for it

- **Your tax bill** — school taxes on a $300,000 home in this district, and
  how much of it leaves under recapture.
- **Where it goes** — teaching versus buildings and debt versus everything
  else, including the annual debt payment expressed in teacher salaries.
- **What each lever actually bought** — measured by comparing districts
  *with themselves* over three years, with a confidence interval on every
  one. Spending $2,000 more per student moves results **+0.15 points, an
  interval that crosses zero.** Where a bar crosses "no effect," the change
  bought nothing that can be told apart from zero.
- **Who does better** — named districts with your size and your share of
  students in poverty that beat expectations *reliably*, over eleven years
  rather than one lucky one. These are phone calls worth making.

## 6. Did the state takeover work? (Houston only)

Houston ISD compared with 13 districts of its size and poverty that were
**not** taken over — because every Texas district was climbing out of the
same pandemic hole, so Houston's own before-and-after settles nothing.
Houston gained **+5.5 points** against the comparison group's **0.0**. The
checks that could break that comparison are shown beside it, including the
one group — special education students — that went backwards.

## 7. Who the district works for

A district average hides who it is failing. This section reports how the
district's **low-income students** do, and where that places them among poor
students statewide.

**It deliberately does not rank districts on the poor/non-poor gap.** Across
Texas that gap correlates about **zero** with how well poor children actually
do, because a gap narrows just as easily when the top falls as when the
bottom rises. The level is the honest measure; the gap is shown as context.

## 8. The debt you voted for

Every bond this district ever put on a ballot — what it was for, what it
cost, and how the vote went. **Hover or tap any circle.** TEA's financial
data does not itemise facilities, so the ballot is the only public record of
what school debt was actually for. Statewide, voters approve classrooms
about 74% of the time and stadiums **48%** of the time.

## 9. Trends, peers and the money breakdown

The trend chart's **gray dashed line is the statewide median**. Peers are
districts closest to your enrollment — your bar is blue, everyone else stays
gray. The money breakdown uses real TEA function codes with instruction
highlighted.

## 10. Anomaly check — flags are questions, not verdicts

If a number jumped in a way the automatic checks find unusual, a card
explains what tripped it and the **innocent explanations to rule out first**
(expired grants, bond programs, boundary changes).

## 11. Take it to a board meeting

- **Print** (top right) — clean printout or save-as-PDF, charts included.
- **Download CSV** — the trend table, ready for Excel or Sheets.
- Every chart also has a plain table behind it, which is what a screen
  reader gets.

## 12. Ask anything

The box near the bottom takes plain English: *"How did our debt service
change since 2020?"* An AI translates it into a query against the same
read-only data. **AI can be wrong** — check anything important against the
charts or [official TEA releases](https://tea.texas.gov/finance-and-grants/state-funding/state-funding-reports-and-data/peims-financial-data-downloads).

## Things to keep in mind

- Test results are at **Meets grade level** unless a label says otherwise.
- Effect sizes are **observational**. Comparing a district with itself over
  time removes everything about it that does not change, but not something
  that changed at the same time. A confidence interval that crosses zero
  means "we cannot tell this apart from no effect" — not "this works a
  little."
- Very small districts show extreme per-student numbers, because they are
  dividing by few students. That is arithmetic, not scandal.
- Tax figures are withheld where two independent estimates of a district's
  tax base disagree by more than 25%. Charter districts levy no property tax
  at all, so they have no bill to show.
- District-level totals only — no student, family or personnel data.
- This is an independent transparency tool, **not an official TEA product**.
  Full disclaimer on the **Disclaimer** button.
